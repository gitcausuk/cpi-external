Centre for Pedagogical Innovation · Oxford Saïd

When the machine can already do the work, what is teaching for?

The Centre for Pedagogical Innovation is how Saïd Business School answers that question with evidence rather than assertion. It is an approved and funded centre. Between Michaelmas 2026 and Hilary 2028 it will run teaching and assessment pilots across the School's programmes, fund faculty research, and evaluate the results independently.

£112,000 has been awarded. Further support would extend how much of the programme runs, how rigorously it is evaluated, and whether the findings travel beyond Oxford.

The case

Three reasons this is worth funding now

CPI's proposition is narrow and testable. It is not a claim that technology improves education, and it is not a plan to buy tools. It is a proposal to find out, carefully, what happens to teaching, assessment and the formation of judgement when capable AI is in the room.

01

Adoption is running ahead of evidence

Business schools worldwide are changing assessment, course design and classroom practice in response to generative AI. CPI's working position is that these changes are being made faster than anyone is measuring what they do to learning.

Very little of the resulting practice is being tested, compared or published. The methods being adopted this year will shape a generation of managers, largely untested.

02

Oxford is an unusually good place to test it

The Oxford model puts the thing at issue directly in view. Tutorials, case discussion and debate ask students to defend, revise and apply their thinking under intellectual pressure, in dialogue, in real time.

That makes the mechanism observable. If AI deepens human reasoning in the room, it should show here. If it flattens or substitutes for it, that should show here too.

03

The centre is already running

CPI is not a proposal seeking start-up capital. Funding is secured, academic sponsorship is in place, and the Founding Director already supports faculty across the School's degree programmes, Executive Education and Oxford Saïd Online.

Publicly accessible resources built by CPI, including the Oxford AI Toolkit, are in use by faculty today. A funder is joining work in progress, not underwriting an idea.

The programme

Six strands, running from 2026 to 2028

Preparatory activity begins in Michaelmas 2026. Formal launch follows in Hilary Term 2027, with the first pilots running by the end of that term. Each strand is described below with what it delivers and what evidence it returns.

Strand 01

Teaching and assessment pilots

Working with faculty to develop, test and evaluate AI-enabled, experiential and assessment-aware approaches in four to six selected modules across the programme portfolio.

Delivers
Redesigned modules taught and evaluated in live conditions.
Evidence returned
Module-level findings, student and faculty evidence, and a documented account of what did not work.

Strand 02

Faculty Champions

An initial peer cohort across academic areas, developing, testing and sharing effective practice. Participation is voluntary and supported through CPI co-design, pedagogical assistance and peer exchange.

Delivers
A cohort established and supported across academic areas.
Evidence returned
Practice case studies, and evidence on how methods move between colleagues rather than staying with one course.

Strand 03

Faculty research and seed grants

Up to six projects supported through awards of £2,500, covering pedagogical research and substantive research in which AI or immersive technology is used as an instrument. The first application call opens in Hilary Term 2027.

Delivers
Up to six funded projects, from a pool of £15,000.
Evidence returned
Project reports, academic outputs, and faculty positioned to apply for larger external research funding.

Strand 04

Context-rich case development

Development of an international cross-cultural case prototype, exploring how immersive, experiential or AI-supported methods can strengthen managerial judgement in context rather than in the abstract.

Delivers
A working case prototype, tested with students.
Evidence returned
Evidence on what transfers from simulated decisions to real ones, and what does not.

Strand 05

Oxford Saïd Forum on Teaching, Learning and AI

A School-facing Forum at which faculty, students, alumni and selected collaborators examine what CPI is learning from its pilots and research. The inaugural Forum is planned for June 2027.

Delivers
An inaugural Forum convened during the funded phase.
Evidence returned
A public account of the findings, and a venue in which they can be tested against practice elsewhere.

Strand 06

Evaluation and sustainability

Proportionate and independent evaluation built into the funded programme, informing decisions about what should be repeated, revised, scaled, researched further or discontinued, and supporting planning beyond the grant period.

Delivers
Independent evaluation across the programme.
Evidence returned
An evaluation report that a funder can rely on, including findings that do not favour CPI.

What comes back

Questions we can actually answer, and reporting you can hold us to

CPI's pilots and seed-grant projects are designed around specific, testable questions. Evaluation is proportionate to each activity: honest judgement about what to keep, not claims of causal proof the evidence cannot support.

Reporting to funders

  1. A short written update each term, covering activity, spend against plan and what has changed.
  2. A mid-point review with the option of a meeting, at which a funder can redirect emphasis.
  3. A final report with data, materials and the independent evaluation findings.
  4. Reporting cadence and format can be aligned to a funder's own requirements.
  5. Negative and null findings are reported, not quietly dropped. This is a condition of the work being worth funding.

Stewardship

How the money is held, spent and accounted for

CPI sits inside Saïd Business School and operates through the School's existing approval and governance processes. Nothing about it is freestanding.

Where funds sit

Gifts and grants are received and administered through the School's established financial processes, under University of Oxford financial regulations. CPI does not hold funds independently.

Who is accountable

The Founding Director is accountable for delivery and reporting, working to a senior sponsor within the School, with four academic co-sponsors providing academic oversight of the research and pilot programme.

Ethics and data

Activity involving students follows University research ethics, information-security and data-protection requirements. Participation by faculty and students in pilots is voluntary.

What is published

Findings, materials and methods are shared with educators across the School so that what works travels beyond the course where it started, and, where appropriate, published externally. Negative findings are included.

Independence from suppliers

CPI does not endorse products. Where a technology company supports the work, findings are published whether or not they favour that company's tools, and support does not confer exclusivity or influence over what is reported.

Coordination

CPI coordinates with programme teams and School functions through established governance, and with University units including the Centre for Teaching and Learning and the University's AI infrastructure, in line with policy and procurement requirements.

What could go wrong

A funder should know the honest risks before committing, so they are set out here rather than discovered later.

  • Uptake is voluntary. Faculty choose whether to pilot. If too few volunteer in a given area, that strand runs smaller than planned.
  • Some pilots will fail. That is the point of running them. A programme where everything succeeds is a programme that was not testing anything.
  • The Nest is not approved. It is subject to School space planning and consultation. CPI can deliver the rest of the programme without it.
  • The technology moves. Tools tested in 2027 may be superseded. The questions are framed around learning and judgement rather than specific products, which is intended to keep the findings useful beyond any one tool.

Where a component underdelivers, the intention is to report it, redirect the remaining resource with the funder's agreement, and say publicly what was learned.

Working with CPI

The terms we work to, and what happens next

These apply to every funder, and they are set out before any conversation rather than negotiated after it.

Findings are published regardless of outcome

Results are reported whether or not they support a funder's interests or prior position. A funder may see findings in advance and respond to them, and is not able to suppress or reframe them.

No endorsement, no exclusivity

CPI does not endorse products or providers. Support does not confer exclusivity, preferential treatment in the classroom, or influence over what is studied or reported.

Restricted and unrestricted support

Support may be directed at a particular strand or given unrestricted. Where it is restricted, we would want to agree a scale at which that strand can produce evidence worth having.

In-kind support

Compute, licences, technical staff time and access to tools can be more useful than funding for the technical strands. In-kind support is treated under the same reporting and independence conditions.

Reporting

Cadence, format and metrics can be aligned to a funder's own requirements, including a specific evaluation framework where one is required.

Acknowledgement

Funders are acknowledged in outputs, on the CPI site and at the Forum, in line with School and University conventions. Recognition beyond standard acknowledgement is a matter for the School rather than for CPI.

Next steps

  1. Step 01An exploratory call

    A short conversation about what you are trying to learn or change, and whether CPI is the right instrument for it. It commits you to nothing.

  2. Step 02A written proposal

    A specific document covering the strands relevant to you, costed, with the evidence each would return and the risks attached.

  3. Step 03Due diligence

    References, financial and governance detail, and any documentation your process requires, coordinated with the School's Development Office.

  4. Step 04Agreement and start

    Terms confirmed in writing through the School, with reporting dates fixed at the outset rather than agreed later.

Component C-07

The Nest

The Nest is CPI's intended physical home: a flexible, shared-use setting where educators try new approaches in low-stakes sessions before bringing them into live teaching, with co-design clinics, peer observation and immersive work in one place.

It is also the part of the work we are most careful about describing. The concept assumes no exclusive occupation, no displacement of current users and no fixed architectural solution. It remains subject to School space planning, consultation and approval, and CPI can deliver the rest of the programme in existing rooms while those plans develop.

We would rather say that plainly now than discover it during due diligence.
